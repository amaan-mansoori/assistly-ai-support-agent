# Decision Log

This log records non-obvious design decisions, trade-offs, and their rationale.
Target: 10–15 entries by project completion.

| # | Decision | Alternatives Considered | Rationale | Date |
|---|----------|------------------------|-----------|------|
| 1 | Use a subsample instead of the full ~3M tweet dataset | Full dataset; streaming processing | Full dataset is unnecessarily large for demonstrating the approach. Subsampling lets us iterate faster, keeps API costs low, and makes reproduction feasible in <15 min. | 2026-09-10 |
| 2 | Project structure mirrors Cookiecutter Data Science conventions | Flat structure; notebook-only | Separating data stages (raw → interim → processed → golden) makes the pipeline reproducible and auditable. Module-based src layout enables unit testing. | 2026-09-10 |
| 3 | Defer brand selection until after data inspection | Pre-select a popular brand like @AppleSupport | Different brands have vastly different conversation volumes, reply rates, and topic diversity. Premature selection risks picking a brand with insufficient or skewed data. | 2026-09-10 |
| 4 | Candidate selection methodology based on 7 operational dimensions | Select solely by volume (e.g. AmazonHelp) or familiarity (e.g. AppleSupport) | Raw volume is deceptive: high-volume accounts often rely on templated 'please DM us' deflections rather than substantive solutions. Evaluating multi-turn threads, substantive resolution signals, issue diversity, and RAG feasibility ensures the selected brand supports a realistic AI agent. Candidates narrowed to AmazonHelp, AppleSupport, and SpotifyCares; final selection pending empirical metric review. | 2026-09-12 |
| 5 | Select AmazonHelp as final brand | AppleSupport, SpotifyCares | Empirical validation across 7 dimensions confirms AmazonHelp is superior for building a grounded AI support agent: 1) Resolution quality: 81.9% substantive resolution rate vs Apple's 52.5% DM deflection and Spotify's 30.9% DM deflection; 2) Dialogue depth: 47.8% multi-turn conversations (>=3 turns) vs Apple (21.1%) and Spotify (27.6%); 3) Usable data: 138,255 verified customer+substantive reply pairs for RAG and few-shot grounding; 4) Quality & diversity: 83.4% high-quality problem descriptions with <0.3% noise and clear issue clusters (shipping, delivery status, Prime membership, order cancellation, refunds, payments). | 2026-09-12 |
| 6 | Empirical intent discovery and English-locale scoping | Pre-defining static intents; multilingual joint modeling | Local language measurement revealed 84.1% English, 7.0% Spanish, 4.3% French, 2.6% German, and 1.3% Japanese. Restricting the core agent to English eliminates multi-language tokenization and evaluation noise while still providing 117,934 cleaned, unique inquiries. Rather than adopting an artificial taxonomy, 12 empirical candidate issue areas were induced via customer TF-IDF n-grams and mapped to historical resolution patterns (tracking guidance, replacement portals, billing adjustments, clarification requests). | 2026-09-12 |
| 7 | Empirically Refined 10-Intent Taxonomy with Operational Boundary Criteria | Keep 13 candidate clusters; lump everything into a catch-all; use zero-shot LLM labels without empirical clustering | Unsupervised clustering (K=8..20) revealed that the 73.76% `general_inquiry_other` bucket was primarily driven by conversational follow-ups (50.44% of all interactions are turns 3/5/7 lacking problem context) and rigid keyword gaps. We isolated conversational dialogue artifacts, merged redundant operational workflows (e.g., merging wrong item with damaged/defective into one return/replacement flow), and split distinct customer journeys (e.g., separating delivery delays from tracking-delivered-missing-parcels). The final 10 task-oriented intents have concrete boundary inclusion/exclusion rules, explicit PII escalation policies, and verified historical Amazon resolution playbooks. | 2026-09-12 |

| 9 | Audit intents with recovered Twitter parent context, while retaining isolated-message results as a baseline | Isolated tweets only; treat follow-up language as an intent | Parent IDs show that many support tweets are conversational replies whose local wording is incomplete. Customer intent, conversation state, and message type must remain separate fields; `thanks`, `sent DM`, and `order number` are not intents. | 2026-09-12 |
| 10 | Resolve the usable substantive customer-pair metric by rerunning the exact raw-data join | Preserve either earlier report value (138,255 or 138,264) | Direct recomputation over AmazonHelp outbound tweets, the existing substantive-response heuristic, and verified `inbound=True` parents yields 138,255. The 138,264 figure is stale/unsupported. | 2026-09-12 |
| 11 | Replace declaration-order delivery classification with an explicit delivered-and-missing predicate | Keep broad delay regex first; merge all delivery issues | A delivered-but-missing report requires both confirmation-of-delivery and non-receipt evidence. Evaluating that predicate before generic delay prevents precedence swallowing. | 2026-09-12 |
| 12 | Keep `other_unclear` and label clustering as exploratory | Force cluster labels into the final schema; claim KMeans topics are intents | Local TF-IDF/KMeans is useful for audit and sampling, but cannot establish customer-intent ground truth. Residual ambiguity should be retained until human annotation. | 2026-09-12 |
| 13 | Perform a second coverage-gap audit only on context-aware `other_unclear` messages | Expand taxonomy to absorb all residual wording; create labels from cluster IDs | The residual must be tested for coherent customer goals separately from message forms such as acknowledgements, contact requests, greetings, and detail submission. New categories are considered only when examples, boundaries, and related historical resolutions support them. | 2026-09-12 |
| 14 | Treat digital/device support and gift-card/promotion issues as candidate additions; broaden product inquiry and merge address changes | Add a generic customer-service intent; treat every product/Prime keyword as a separate intent | These groups have potentially distinct remedies, whereas contact-channel language is commonly routing metadata and shipping-address changes share the order-change workflow. All changes remain pending human annotation. | 2026-09-12 |
